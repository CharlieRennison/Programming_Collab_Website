Health care

How Remote Computing benefits society

Authored by Shang kai Huang Linkedin Github

Introduction

Computing has transformed healthcare from paper work into an online high precision field. By connecting doctor around the world and bring healthcare into people home, computer science is actively saving people lives and improving the accuracte diagnosis of diseases.

Remote computing helping health care

Remote computing has help improve patient outcome, lower costs and help streamline operation. It allows rural patient to get access to health care by connecting with medical professional without travelling long distance. According to the NHS colon cancer kills 30k people per year due to late diagonostics and the current waiting list is 500k people, so the benefit of remote computing is to help diagnostic severe condition which is critial to reducing the waiting list.

Remote computing helps with cost savings

A comprehensive research paper from the Australian productivity commision calculated that implementing digital software technologies has saved over $5 billions anuually, which can be spent on other issue or technology to improve the overall health care system. Furthermore, survey published by the American medical association found that up to 70% of patient using virtual on demand care would have other wise gone to an expensive Emergency Department or Urgent Care facility. Transitioning these acute non-urgent episodes to telehealth generated average saving of 6% or $242 per care episode.

Healthcare workforce efficiency

Administrative loads contribute heavily to medical burnout. Reports show that integrating clinical AI and remote computing automation can successfully manage up to 30% of a healthcare worker's routine data and administrative tasks. This saves an average of 11 hours per week per health worker, allowing that time to be redirected entirely back into direct patient care. Furthermore, according to McMaster health forum Studies show that utilizing ambient digital scribes cuts the time spent writing appointment notes by 20.4% and slashes daily Electronic Health Record (EHR) data-entry tasks by roughly 20 minutes per day. Beyond saving time, these tools capture clinical conversations with extreme precision. Data reveals that this comprehensive capturing allows for more accurate coding intensity. Large medical networks use this automation to review complex charts, with major providers projecting up to $400 million in operational and billing lifecycle savings.
